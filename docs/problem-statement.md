# Problem Statement

## Who is affected?
Students, tutors, and administrators who use the Smart Online Tutoring Management System (SOTMS).

## What exactly is going wrong?
Managing tutoring activities can involve multiple tasks, including finding tutors, booking sessions, processing payments, tracking upcoming lessons, and managing learning materials. Without an integrated system, these activities may be difficult to coordinate efficiently.

## Why does it matter?
Inefficient tutoring management can cause confusion about bookings, delays in communication, difficulties tracking sessions, and additional administrative work. Students may also find it harder to review their learning progress and reinforce their understanding after lessons.

## Problem statement (3 to 5 sentences)
Students, tutors, and administrators need an efficient way to manage online tutoring activities. Coordinating tutor selection, session bookings, payments, notifications, and learning materials can be time-consuming when these activities are not integrated into one system. This can lead to booking confusion, communication delays, and additional administrative work. Students may also lack convenient access to personalized learning summaries and quizzes after tutoring sessions. Therefore, SOTMS aims to integrate tutoring management and AI-based learning support into one platform.

## Supporting Evidence

The Asian Development Bank (2023) reviewed online learning experiences among higher education students in six Asian countries. The report examined challenges involving internet availability, student satisfaction, student–teacher interaction, and the need for technical and learning support. This evidence highlights the importance of providing appropriate support for online learners. SOTMS aims to address related needs by integrating tutoring management, session notifications, and personalized learning support into one platform.

**Reference:** Asian Development Bank. (2023). *Online Learning during the COVID-19 Pandemic: A Review of Student Experiences in Asian Higher Education.*
